CRD6, branches of third-country banks

On 11 January 2027, a new regime applies to branches in France of banks established outside the European Union. It sets capital, liquidity, governance and reporting requirements. The rest of the CRD6 Directive has applied since 11 January 2026.

The CRD6 Directive harmonises the regime for branches of third-country banks. In France, it is transposed by Ordinance No 2026-255 of 8 April 2026, Decree No 2026-309 of 24 April 2026 and an order of 29 July 2026. The provisions on the provision of banking services by third-country undertakings follow a deferred timetable and apply on 11 January 2027.

The ACPR presented the regime as early as October 2025: ring-fenced capital endowment, liquidity ratio, policy for booking transactions, power to require a subsidiary. From 11 January 2027 it also applies the EBA guidelines on the instruments that cover the risks or losses of the branch.

Our view

A branch has no legal balance sheet of its own: its accounts are a view of the head office. The new regime requires knowing, transaction by transaction, what is booked in the branch, and producing capital, liquidity and reporting figures at that level.

What we do

Gap analysis, rules for booking transactions in the branch, production of accounting and prudential reports, reconciliations with head-office accounts.
