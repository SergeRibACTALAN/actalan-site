Open banking, Section 1033

In the United States, the rule requiring banks to share their customers’ financial data, at their request, is suspended. The CFPB is rewriting it. The initial deadlines, from 2026 to 2030, no longer apply as they stood.

Four points under reconsideration

Initial deadlines

From 1 April 2026 to 1 April 2030, depending on the size of the institution.

The Personal Financial Data Rights rule, published on 18 November 2024, requires institutions to make their customers’ data available to them and to authorised third parties. It provided for staggered deadlines from 1 April 2026 to 1 April 2030, depending on the size of the institution.

The CFPB opened the reconsideration of the rule on 22 August 2025, on four points: representation of the customer, fees, data security and data privacy. A federal court first stayed the deadlines by 90 days; according to several law firms, it then barred the CFPB from enforcing the rule until the reconsideration is complete. A draft new rule has been under review since 4 August 2026.

Our view

The direction of the new rule remains uncertain, particularly on fees. What will not change: exposing customer data to third parties requires interfaces, recorded consents and clean data. European institutions know the subject through payment services regulation.
