GENIUS Act, payment stablecoins

On 18 January 2027, the GENIUS Act and the OCC final rule take effect. To issue a payment stablecoin in the United States, one must in principle be a permitted issuer. The Act governs reserves, redemption, capital and custody.

The GENIUS Act, enacted on 18 July 2025, creates a federal framework for payment stablecoins. It takes effect on 18 January 2027, as does the OCC final rule implementing it for entities within its jurisdiction, including national banks and federal branches of foreign banks.

From that date, one must in principle be approved as a permitted issuer to issue a payment stablecoin in the United States. An issuer whose application is substantially complete by 18 January 2027 may request a waiver, limited to twelve months. From 18 July 2028, digital asset service providers will no longer be allowed to offer stablecoins from non-permitted issuers.

Our view

For a European group, the question arises through its US entities and through the dollar stablecoins it uses or holds in custody for its clients. It connects with the accounting treatment of crypto-assets in Europe: nature of the asset, reserves, holders’ rights.
